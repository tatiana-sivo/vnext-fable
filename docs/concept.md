# vNext Clean Room Concept
## Independent product concept from first-principles analysis

Generated by giving an AI model (Fable 5) the full vNext problem space with all solutions stripped out. Three sessions: concept architecture, coordination model, then critique response with revisions. This document combines and edits all three for readability. The originals are preserved separately.

**The one-sentence product definition:** vNext exists so a marketer can place fewer, provable bets and hand each one to AI at exactly the level of trust it has earned.

---

# Part 1: Critique of the Problem Framing

Before designing anything, the model interrogated the problem space itself. These findings stand independent of the concept that follows.

## The five headline jobs and the eight job clusters disagree

Trust, proof, distinctiveness, effort, AEO - the five headline jobs are externally legible and AI-era-coded. But the document's own numbers contradict their ranking:
- 55% cite connecting marketing to revenue as their #1 gap - buried in Cluster B
- Revenue/sales alignment and data quality are called the biggest gaps in current thinking - neither makes the top five
- AEO has 700 signups and 1.6% publish rate - elevated by novelty, not by evidence of decision-relevance for the base

**Pattern:** jobs that are exciting to talk about outrank jobs the data says are more painful.

## "Agent proposes, human approves" was smuggled into the problem statement

Job 1 reads: "The agent proposes at volume; the marketer needs to stand behind what goes out." That's an interaction model stated as if it were the problem. A solution-free version would say: "the marketer must be able to trust output at the volume AI produces it" - and leave the mechanism open.

## Missing jobs

- **Contain a mistake that already shipped.** Every job in the brief is pre-publish. Nothing covers the moment AI output is live and wrong: who's notified, what gets pulled, how fast, what the fix looks like to the person who saw it.
- **Legal and compliance exposure.** Distinct from brand risk. AI at scale creates real exposure - false claims, IP collision, consent violations. This is binary and gate-based, not graduated and subjective like brand judgment. Collapsing them is why "review" gets treated as one generic behavior everywhere.
- **Forecasting.** Job 2 is entirely backward-looking (did it work). Nobody asked for forward-looking: if I move $50K from paid social to AEO, what happens to pipeline next quarter? A marketer building a budget case for a CFO needs a scenario model, not a report card.
- **Reacting to the unplanned.** Every job assumes steady-state cycles. Nothing covers the trending moment, the competitor crisis, the news hook. The one place AI's speed advantage should matter most.
- **"What am I for now."** Underneath trust resistance sits an identity question. If the agent executes, what is the marketer's job? Some resistance coded as "quality concerns" is actually this. Left unnamed, it presents as a trust problem and behaves as an existential one.
- **Managing AI spend.** The moment vNext ships on credits, every customer acquires this job. The brief treats credits as a business constraint, not a customer job.

## Embedded assumptions worth challenging

- **That trust is built through transparency.** Automation-trust research says otherwise: trust comes from calibrated reliability and cheap recoverability. People don't read reasoning; they check track records and whether mistakes are reversible.
- **That the campaign survives as the unit of work.** The brief documents its death (48.2% wrappers) then keeps reasoning in campaign terms.
- **That context must be pulled INTO HubSpot.** The research shows work lives in Slack, docs, ChatGPT. HubSpot as a headless context provider that shows up in those places is never considered - even though the Clay/Jasper disintermediation risk is named.
- **That "develop marketing strategy" means a missing tool.** In SMBs, strategy isn't a distinct activity done in a tool. It's decisions made inside execution moments that then evaporate. The gap might be missing memory, not a missing surface.
- **That HubSpot's data advantage is the customer's own data.** For a 30-person company, portal data is thin and dirty. The real asset is the 280K-portal benchmark - network knowledge about what normal and good look like. The brief calls this "potential" and moves on. Klaviyo built its entire pitch on the equivalent.
- **That priority order is global.** It inverts by segment. A solo marketer needs "do with less effort" ranked far higher than a 500-person CMO does.

## Jobs to split, merge, or reframe

- **Split Job 1** into: (a) quality/brand confidence (graduated, subjective), (b) legal/compliance safety (binary, gate-based), (c) calibration over time ("when do I stop reviewing?"). Different mechanisms for each.
- **Split Job 2** into: "know what's working" (fast, operational, feeds optimization) and "defend the budget" (translated into narrative, built to survive a skeptical CFO). Different audiences, cadences, rigor bars.
- **Merge Clusters A and C.** Strategy and Revenue/Sales Alignment are not peers - deciding what to invest in without co-owned targets is irresponsible. Treating them as separate is plausibly why both are underserved.
- **Reframe Cluster A** from "develop strategy" to "make decisions that persist and compound." The gap isn't a planning canvas; it's that decisions have no home, no memory, no consequences.

# Part 2: The Concept

## Organizing principle: commitments reconciled against reality

Rejected alternatives:
- **Agents as organizing principle** (Salesforce): 12% adoption is the verdict
- **Chat**: converged, commodity, no persistence
- **Campaigns**: dead per the data (48% wrappers)
- **Goals alone**: closest, but goals don't hold brand/ICP/definitions - the durable truths that outlive any quarter. And goal containers can become wrappers if intent-setting is homework.

What unifies the decide/prove/align/position jobs: they are all commitments. A target is a commitment to a number. An ICP is a commitment to a market. A brand system is a commitment to a voice. An MQL definition is a commitment shared with sales. Proof is reconciling reality against commitments. Trust in AI is delegated commitment-keeping.

## The object model

| Object | What it is | What it replaces |
|---|---|---|
| **Position** | Durable truth, versioned and co-signed: ICP/segment definitions, brand system, positioning, MQL/handoff definitions, channel theses. Changes slowly and deliberately, with a decision record. Subscribable via MCP - other tools read FROM HubSpot. | ICP/context fragmented across 11+ tools |
| **Bet** | Time-bound commitment with a number, an owner, a budget (money AND credits), and a falsification condition ("40 fintech demos by Sep 30; if <20 by Aug 15, we kill it"). Pipeline Bets carry two signatures - marketing and sales. | Campaign-as-empty-folder, goals-as-aspiration |
| **Play** | Typed execution unit (re-engagement, launch, webinar promo, nurture) with network-benchmarked outcome ranges and credit cost ranges from the 280K-portal corpus. Minimum three fields to instantiate: audience (a Position), Bet it ladders to, and budget. | Loose assets with no intent above them |
| **Ledger** | Per-Bet business-clock proof: target vs actual. Append-only record of every decision, delegation grant, credit spent, outcome. | Fragmented manual reporting |
| **Signal** | Portfolio-level brand-clock health: trend vs benchmark. No targets, no progress bars. | Brand health with no home |
| **Close** | Weekly ritual: what shipped, what it cost, what came back, what needs a decision, what the system recommends killing. Quarterly version reads the brand clock and compiles the leadership narrative. | Status meetings, manual reporting decks |

**The structural move that makes measurement work: lineage by default.** Everything gets a guessed attribution at birth - silent, no prompt, no blank field. A reactive LinkedIn post ships instantly; the system files it under the fintech Bet as a provisional fact. Correcting a guess costs seconds. Authoring an intent costs minutes. That difference is the whole game.

- Provisional edges get confirmed in batch at the Close. "Six items auto-filed under fintech. Right?" One tap. Pressure moves from ship-time (where it blocks flow) to review-time (where it's just information).
- Unattributed work is allowed. It shows up as a visible bucket with a cost line.
- The hard gate survives in one place: AI spend at scale. Humans can ship their own email with no Bet. The AI cannot spend 40 credits on an unattributed sequence. Human reactive freedom stays. Machine spend is always accountable.

**Campaign is demoted.** What HubSpot calls a Campaign today either folds into a Bet's Plays or becomes a Play-grouping convenience beneath a Bet. It stops being the top-level strategic object. A 48% wrapper rate is an indictment of Campaign-as-currently-scoped, not a UX problem fixable within it.

## Revenue and sales alignment - specific mechanism

**Data joining:** The reason "connect spend to pipeline" hasn't been solved isn't a missing integration - HubSpot already has spend, contacts, deals, and campaigns in one database. It's a missing linking object with a time horizon and audience. A Bet supplies that. Once spend and Plays are scoped to a Bet with a defined audience and window, the Ledger's spend-to-pipeline number is a query across tables that already exist.

**Sales alignment:** A Bet can have a sales co-owner. MQL/SQL threshold definitions live once in a Position - shared, not separately held. The Ledger for any Bet surfaces as a panel on the Deal record inside the CRM, where sales already works. Pipeline reviews pull the same Ledger data into the existing pipeline-review surface. A generated weekly digest goes to both owners over whatever channel they already use.

This solves the surfacing half. It does not solve the incentive half - a sales VP has no structural reason to co-define thresholds just because the product makes it easy.

## Priority tiers

**Tier 1 - Positions and the Bet/Ledger spine (Clusters A, B, D).** Where the coverage inversion says HubSpot is weakest. Every other cluster is downstream - content, brand, and measurement are only as good as the ICP, voice, and positioning definitions under them. Open differentiation window: no competitor has solved persistent intent objects.

**Tier 2 - Revenue/Sales Alignment (C) and Brand governance (G).** Must be designed into the Bet/Ledger schema from day one - co-ownership, shared definitions, deal-record embedding are schema decisions, not features bolted on later. Brand governance (voice and positioning in Positions) must exist before Move generation scales, or the product generates a mountain of off-brand content and retrofits taste. Brand measurement (Signal) can lag a quarter.

**Tier 3 - Content (E), Channels (F), Orchestration (H).** E and F are commoditizing - every competitor is racing here, HubSpot already has adoption. Build to parity, not past it. H is mostly an information-architecture consolidation problem.

**AEO does not get a dedicated surface.** It folds into Positions as one more fit-check a Play is scored against. The 1.6% publish rate reads as evidence the job isn't decision-relevant yet for most of the base.

## The two clocks - enforced structurally

- **Business clock lives on Bets**: targets, pace-vs-plan, attribution to Plays. Progress bars. Read weekly at the Close.
- **Brand clock lives on Positions**: trend vs 280K benchmark, health indicators. No targets, no progress bars. Read quarterly. The cadence itself encodes the physics - you can't anxiously refresh a number that only renders quarterly.
- **The promotion mechanism**: a brand metric earns a target only through an explicit, argued, logged link. The AI's role is to argue AGAINST weak promotion claims. This makes the "argued link" rule a product mechanism, not a guideline.

## Credits shape the concept

- **Credit quote as explanation.** Every AI job presents a bid before running: scope, credit cost, expected outcome range, benchmark basis ("400-contact re-engagement, ~12 credits, expected 8-15 recoveries based on plays like yours"). Frame AI work as media spend, not software usage. Approval becomes "fund this bet" - a decision marketers already know how to make.
- **Credits enter the attribution model.** Every credit spent is attributed to outcomes in the same system that attributes ad spend. "AI ROI" becomes automatic. The AI gets a P&L line that can go negative, visibly. That's a forcing function on HubSpot to ship only AI that pays back.
- **Thinking is cheap, shipping at scale is metered.** Drafts, plans, analysis, quotes cost little or nothing. Deployment at volume costs credits. Metering exploration is how you guarantee the 82% never start.
- **Credits are budgeted per Bet** with auto-stop rules. Allocated like media, not consumed like a utility.
- **Pruning has negative cost.** Surfacing "credits reclaimed by pruning" turns killing a bad Bet from an admission of failure into good portfolio hygiene.
- **Credit warranty (year two).** At Bet creation, the agent states a confidence range. If the Bet runs faithfully and results fall outside the range, a portion of credits are refunded. Puts HubSpot's margin against its AI's calibration. Needs actuarial work before shipping, but the schema should capture confidence ranges from day one.

## Serving the 82% without a mode split

- **Non-delegation value is complete on its own.** Positions, Bets, the Close, benchmarks, the sales contract - none require trusting an agent with anything. A marketer who only reads the weekly Close and co-signs the MQL contract is getting the core value.
- **Cold-start is a gift, not homework.** Day one, the system drafts your Positions from your own portal: ICP from closed-won patterns, voice from top-performing content, definitions from existing lifecycle stages. First contact is "it already knows us," not "configure me." The human corrects a draft, never fills a blank page.
- **Progression is micro, per lane, prompted by track record.** "You've approved 47 subject lines unedited. Stop reviewing this class?" No mode toggle. Frontier users grant fast; the 82% may stay at full manual forever and remain happy customers.
- **AI shows up where 91% of adoption already happens** - asset surfaces and Slack - not in a new hub.

## The human-AI relationship model

Not one universal model. Different mechanisms for different kinds of work:

| Mechanism | When it applies | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Co-authoring** | High-stakes, low-frequency: Bet hypotheses, Position definitions | Human and AI edit the same live artifact. No discrete accept/reject. AI fills empty slots as ghost text; locks out of human-touched slots. |
| **Propose / approve** | Rare, high-stakes single decisions: large paid-media commitment, press-facing campaign | AI drafts, human explicitly accepts or rejects. Doesn't create review fatigue because it's rare. |
| **Constrain, then execute** | Routine execution inside an approved Bet and Position | Review happens once, upstream. Execution auto-clears if it passes deterministic checks. No per-item review. |
| **Delegate, then audit** | Task types that have earned delegated trust via track record | AI executes automatically. Human reviews a rotating sample after the fact, can revoke instantly. |

A fifth mode runs underneath all four: **AI as critic.** Evaluates output against Positions - voice, distinctiveness, compliance - regardless of who produced it, human or agent. Can compare a draft against an anonymized aggregate of what 280K other portals are currently producing in the same category, flagging convergence (everyone's AI is writing the same thing). Applies the same bar to human-written work as agent-written work.

**The prosecutor** is embedded in artifacts people already touch, not a mutable notification channel:

- **The standing answer's risk line.** Every Bet's one-line account includes "biggest open risk," computed, with receipts. You can't mute the status mechanism itself.
- **The narrative compiler.** The quarterly leadership story won't emit a claim that failed its own falsification pass without attaching the counterevidence. You read your own report, which was already cross-examined before you saw it.
- **Promotion gates.** A brand metric earns a target only by surviving the argued-link challenge. Gates don't have mute buttons.

The interactive prosecutor - the one that argues with you directly - stays mutable, and that's fine. It's the bonus tier. Mutes are scoped to flag classes and logged in the Ledger. High dismiss rates on a flag class auto-lower its volume - mute-worthiness is treated as the prosecutor's quality bug, not the user's attitude problem.

# Part 3: How Humans and AI Coordinate Work

Three principles govern all coordination:

1. **State lives on objects. Every surface is a mirror that writes back.** The editor, the Delta, a Slack card - all views of the same signed object. No decision state ever lives in a channel.
2. **Everything you're shown is a diff against something you signed.** Never a feed, never a full document. Catch-up, approvals, status - all diffs against commitments.
3. **Two earned currencies.** Autonomy = permission to act without asking. Attention = permission to ask without queueing. Both are granted, tracked, and calibrated from the Ledger. Both can shrink. The collateral: reversibility is engineered before action (holds, waves, tripwires), not improvised after.

## Sync: who has the cursor

The human. Always. The AI never holds the cursor - it has slots and margins.

Two collaboration models, matched to the artifact type:

**Typed objects (Positions, Bets, Plays)** use slot-level turn-taking:

- **Empty slots**: AI drafts ahead as ghost text. Tab accepts, Esc rejects. Ignorable by construction.
- **Human-touched slots**: locked to the AI. It can only annotate in the margin, and only re-enters on new information, stating what the information is. Never re-proposes rejected content.
- **Contested slots**: the AI disagrees on record, visibly, without blocking.
- **Turn boundaries are field commits, not keystrokes.** When you commit a field, downstream proposals update quietly (a counter, not visible rewriting). Chat is a side thread attached to the object - it never silently mutates the artifact.

**Prose (email body, blog posts, social copy)** uses span ownership and tracked changes:

- Anything the human typed or edited is human-owned. AI never touches a human span except as a visible tracked change.
- Turns are explicit, diffs are mandatory. "Tighten the intro" arrives as tracked changes scoped to the intro. Everything else is structurally frozen. Regeneration doesn't exist as an operation.
- Constraints anchor the margins. The brand system and claims Positions travel with the doc. Margin comments cite them ("this claim isn't in the approved list"), not style opinions.
- The typed shell (subject line, preheader, CTA, audience) stays slots with ghost text. The body is diff-governed prose.

By trust level: L0 (manual) - ghost text off; margins on request only; it's a good structured editor. L1 (review-required) - the default above. L2 (delegated) - AI may auto-commit trivial slots (metadata, benchmark citations); substantive slots still ghost.

Credits: the cursor is never metered. Ghost drafting, forking, margin work - free. The only credit event is execution downstream. Metering the co-author kills it.

If AI is mediocre: bad ghost text doesn't get accepted and the tool degrades to a structured editor with comments. Still a working product. AI contributions must be additive to a functioning manual path, never load-bearing.

## Async: what "catching up" looks like

**The Delta** - a diff since you last looked, ranked by actionability, not recency. Same structure as the Close (shipped / cost / returned / needs you), giving you a fractal: a Delta is a continuous Close; the Close is the weekly Delta. One mental model, three zoom levels.

Two mechanisms inside it:

- **Decision-class tags.** Every autonomous action carries its boundary status: "committed under grant #14" / "deferred - outside grant" / "deferred - confidence below threshold." Catching up isn't reading actions; it's scanning whether the AI stayed inside the lines. The header does the work: "23 actions, all inside granted lanes. 2 deferrals waiting."
- **Ranked by remaining agency, not recency.** An auto-sent email still inside its 24h unsend window outranks anything already immutable - because you can still act on it.

Plus: **calibration sampling.** At L2 the system periodically surfaces a random sample of actions it didn't have to show you. Keeps judgment fresh, keeps the track record honest, prevents "delegated" from becoming "blind."

Credits: overnight work spends against per-Bet budgets. The Delta shows spend in the same strip ("14 of 60 credits this week"). Deferring a decision costs no credits, but deferrals carry an honest decay estimate in outcome terms ("each week of delay, expected recoveries drop ~8%, cohort-based"). Hard rule: credits meter AI labor; human attention is never metered. Charging for review would incentivize rubber-stamping.

## Handoffs in both directions

**Human to AI:** You don't configure delegation. You edit and countersign the AI's proposed terms. Point at scope ("run with this" on a Play). The AI returns its bid: scope, which Positions bind it, credit cost, expected range, what it will do without asking, what it will come back for, and the undo story. You edit the boundary lines, then sign. Mid-flight scope drift (>X% over bid) auto-pauses and returns as a change order.

**AI to human:** Attention is bid for, same as execution.

- **Interrupt budget.** Hard cap on pings (default: one per day outside the Delta). Everything else queues silently. Scarcity forces the AI to spend interrupts wisely.
- **Stakes are computed, not claimed.** An interrupt carries what's needed, blast radius, and cost-of-waiting - all derived from object properties. The model cannot inflate its own urgency.
- **Channel is the signal.** Queued = Delta. Time-critical (irreversibility window closing) = one Slack ping. Nothing is labeled URGENT.
- **Attention calibrates both ways.** Dismiss interrupts consistently and the budget shrinks. Keep finding queued items that should have pinged you and the budget grows.
- **Deferral is a first-class reply**: "not now" and "stop asking about this class" (tighten the lane, not the instance).

By trust level: L0 - observations only, near-zero interrupts. L1 - queue delivered in two daily batches, not streamed; flow protected by batching. L2 - interrupts are rare and mostly boundary conditions ("audience matched 3x bid scope; need re-auth").

## Multi-party: a scenario end to end

Structural rules:
- State lives on the object; Slack cards are mirrors with buttons that write back
- Visibility follows signatures - you see what you've signed, been named on, or are bound by
- Disagreement is a state, not a thread: a contested object operates under the last ratified version (old terms govern until new ones are signed); both parties receive the same evidence
- Trust levels are per-relationship, not per-object: Gabby can be L2 on a Play where legal is a permanent L0 gate

**The scenario.** Q3 fintech pipeline Bet. Gabby (marketing) owns it, Dana (sales) co-signed. Pacing behind.

1. Under Gabby's delegated nurture grant, AI drafts a re-engagement Play: 400 stalled fintech contacts, gift-card incentive. Bid: 12 credits, 8-15 expected recoveries, staggered waves, 24h holds. No spend yet.

2. Routing computes signers from rules, not org chart. Gabby: inside her grant, audit only. Legal: hard gate (the incentive clause matched the promotions Position legal co-signed at setup). Dana: notified, not gating.

3. Legal's Slack card shows only what concerns them - the incentive clause and audience jurisdictions - with the matched rule cited. Approve / block / approve-with-edit. Legal edits: cap at EUR 50. The edit writes back to the object; Ledger logs it.

4. System checks whether legal's edit invalidates the bid. Cost unchanged, no re-auth needed. Had outcomes moved materially, the bid would bounce back to Gabby as a change order.

5. Dana's Delta speaks his language: "~34 recovered contacts entering your queue Thursday."

6. Wave 1 sends. Two contacts turn out to be mid-deal. Dana flags "marketing touched my live deal." The system locates the disagreement at the governing artifact - the definitions contract, whose "stalled" definition didn't check CRM freshness. Both sides get the identical evidence. They amend the definition (freshness check added). Decision record logged. AI pauses wave 2, re-filters, resumes. The mistake ended as a one-line tightening of the contract.

**When humans don't behave (degraded paths):**

- **Legal sits on a gate for 3 days.** Gate never auto-unblocks. Instead, work reshapes around the blockage: AI proposes splitting the Play, shipping waves without the incentive clause now, holding that variant for legal. Gate latency is Ledger data shown at the Close ("legal median response 2.8 days, three Plays reshaped around it").
- **Nobody knows who to ping.** Every gate requires a backup chain at signing time. Unroutable gates escalate to the Bet owner as "blocked, no valid approver." Visible and stuck beats silent and stuck.
- **Dana doesn't notice until the deal is lost.** Contract clauses should be executable checks - "stalled" includes a CRM-freshness test at dispatch, so mid-deal contacts get caught by the machine. Human catches are the fallback, never the primary defense.
- **The amendment creates edge cases.** Amendments get canaries. A changed definition runs in shadow first: "under the new 'stalled,' 240 contacts drop out, 90 enter, 3 are mid-deal." Rules get waves, not just messages.
- **The org where nobody signs anything.** The concept degrades to marketing-side lineage, proof, and brand tools, with unsigned contracts as marketing's documented assumptions. Co-sign adoption should ride a live dispute (an MQL rejection fight, where signing is cheaper than arguing), not an onboarding flow.

General rule: the system keeps working in degraded mode, bounds damage without anyone's cooperation, and makes organizational friction legible. It never tries to fix politics with notifications.

## The status problem: Wednesday, CMO asks

The CMO isn't asking "what changed" (that's the Delta). She's asking for a defensible account of now.

**The standing answer.** Every Bet maintains a one-line account, computed from Ledger + telemetry, never hand-authored: pace vs plan, spend vs budget (money and credits), biggest open risk, next decision date - with evidence expandable underneath. The workspace is a stack of standing answers. It reads as a briefing, not charts.

Nobody writes a status update. Status is a byproduct of the objects being real.

Wednesday: Gabby opens workspace, forwards two standing-answer cards to the CMO in Slack, adds one sentence. Or the CMO queries the system directly and gets the same answers, because standing answers are object properties, not reports someone runs. One version of reality for everyone with visibility.

Two safeguards protect the owner:

- **Owner-first windows.** New risk flags land in the owner's Delta first. The shared standing answer updates after the owner has had a working day, or has acknowledged. One reality, sequenced publication. Exception: critical tripwires go to everyone at once.
- **Judgment on record.** The owner's read is the headline of the card; computed facts are evidence beneath it. The CMO reads Gabby's judgment first, numbers second. Over quarters, the owner accumulates a calibration record ("called the fintech risk in week 3, was right") - standing that no manually assembled deck ever built.

Credits: reading status is always free. Generating it is edition-tier, not credits. Status is the product's nervous system; metering it would recreate the fragmented-reporting problem vNext exists to solve. It's also the 82%'s trust on-ramp - the first value they get before delegating anything.

## Accountability and undo

**Accountability follows signatures. Every signature is scoped.**

When AI executes a delegated lane, a teammate approves output in Slack, and it goes out wrong:

- **The grant** (Gabby signed): she owns the decision that this class of work could run without her. Wrong because the lane was too wide - hers.
- **The gate approval** (teammate signed): they own what they were shown - scoped to the diff on their card. If the error lived outside that diff, it's a disclosure fault belonging to the system, not the approver.
- **The bid** (the system signed): HubSpot owns execution matching its own terms. Out-of-bid execution auto-refunds credits and logs as a system fault.

Remediation always ends in one line tightened on one artifact - a lane, a Position, a routing rule. Never "revoke all autonomy."

**Undo mechanics.** You can't unsend email. But you can structure sends so mistakes are catchable and bounded. An action class cannot reach L2 without all three:

- **Waves** - never 100% at once. Wave 1 is a canary (~10%).
- **Hold windows** - messages queue before dispatch. Undo inside the hold is a true undo, zero recipient impact.
- **Tripwires** - bounce/unsub/complaint anomalies auto-pause remaining waves.

**Three common failure modes:**

| Failure | Immediate response | Root-cause fix |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong audience (active deals, wrong segment) | Recall undispatched via holds, auto-pause waves, context alert to deal owners | Tighten segment definition or freshness rule in the Position |
| Wrong content (broken link, off-brand, factual error) | Same hold/pause spine; fix the landing page or redirect; three graded correction protocols (silent fix / correction note / retraction) | Tighten the claims Position or critic checklist |
| Wrong volume/timing (double-send, frequency abuse) | Platform-level frequency governor no lane can override; dedupe at dispatch; deliverability tripwires quarantine | Tighten the cap or scheduler rule |

**Trust is expressed as physics.** L1 means long holds and small waves. Track record shortens holds and widens waves. Autonomy isn't a toggle - it's literally how much of the world the AI can touch before you'd have caught it.

Credits: the meter never runs during incident response. Pausing, suppressing, drafting corrections - free, even when the mistake traces to a human's over-wide grant.

## The economic constitution (five rules)

1. Credits meter AI labor. Human attention is never metered.
2. Exploration and co-authoring are free. Shipping at scale is metered.
3. AI bids for execution in credits and for attention against a budgeted interrupt allowance - both with computed, uninflatable stakes.
4. Deferral is free but shows honest decay.
5. Incident response is never metered. Out-of-bid execution refunds itself.

# Part 4: Stress Test

## Jobs this concept solves weakly

- **Revenue/sales alignment (C).** Co-ownership and shared definitions solve the adoption-friction half. They don't solve the incentive half - a sales VP has no structural reason to co-define thresholds just because the product makes it easy. This cluster's hardest part is the one no interface fixes.
- **AEO / discovery (Job 5).** Deliberately didn't build it a home. If AEO matters as much as vendor conversation suggests by 2027, folding it into a Position fit-check will look like underinvestment.
- **Raw content craft (E).** Deliberately conceded. A team that hires for beautiful creative will find the output competent, not inspired.
- **Speed-to-first-campaign.** Klaviyo's zero-input prompt-to-campaign demo will beat the three-field minimum every time on stage. Betting the demo loser wins the renewal.
- **Agency and multi-portal workflows.** Not addressed. Scoped Bet-level access is a patch, not architecture.
- **Deep approval matrices at 500+ seats.** Blast-radius routing is elegant for mid-market and simplistic for enterprises with legal/regional chains.

## Where a competitor beats this

- **Adobe** is already GA with one-pass goal-to-campaign. Less ceremony, more magical on day one.
- **Salesforce** has deeper model partnerships (OpenAI, Anthropic) and may simply run on better raw capability sooner.
- **Klaviyo** has 14 years of outcome data across 193K brands. The anti-slop fingerprinting mechanism is a new build for HubSpot; Klaviyo could extend an existing pipeline to a similar claim faster.
- **Meta**, from below. For social-first micro-SMBs, Meta automating targeting and creative removes the need for orchestration entirely. That segment churns regardless of concept.

## What has to be true about AI

- **Extraction accuracy** from unstructured docs into Position objects must be good enough that marketers trust it without heavy correction. Mediocre extraction turns ingestion into more cleanup work than it saves.
- **Calibration.** Stated confidence must track reality, not just sound fluent. Required for trust-level graduation and the credit warranty. Current models are known to be overconfident.
- **Long-horizon consistency.** A Bet can run for months. Late-stage execution must stay consistent with the original hypothesis.

**If none of this holds for two years**, the concept degrades rather than breaks. Trust levels default to manual and review-required. The marketer never graduates - and still gets a better-structured intent and proof system than she has today, because Positions/Bets/Ledger/Close are a better information architecture independent of AI quality.

## 3 months vs 12 months

**3 months:** The retroactive Close (compile last quarter from existing portal data as if the Loop had been running - implied bets, benchmarked performance, the things that didn't pay, leadership narrative with receipts). MQL/handoff telemetry from existing CRM data. Credit quotes on existing agents. Positions v0 auto-drafted from portal. Minimal Bets as confirmation targets ("this is 70% right - confirm these six groupings and next quarter is 95% right and takes zero").

**Cut for now:** Weekly Close ritual. Co-sign UX. Trust ladder beyond L0/L1. Plays library. Signal (brand clock). Anti-slop fingerprinting. Credit warranty. CRM-native Ledger embedding in deal records.

The 3-month cut leads with proof: the first-week moment is forwarding a reconstructed Q2 story that would have taken two days to build by hand, with counterevidence already attached. Discipline gets purchased with visible value, not required as homework.

## The strongest argument against this concept

**The frame is "extraction engine with a ratification core."** Not a discipline product, and not discipline-as-pure-byproduct. Roughly 95% of structure is inferred from work. The 5% that must be human-authored - a handful of forward numbers per quarter, two co-signatures, the brand claims - is the minimum for proof and alignment, requested at moments where value is already on the table, not at setup. Three confirmed Bets per quarter is enough skeleton.

The entry sequence: Ledger first (record what's happening), infer structure, then ask for ratification. The product's first act is taking work off her desk, not adding to it. It manufactures headroom before it asks for anything.

Why the 5% ratification core can't be eliminated: proof needs a pre-registered claim (baselines can't be backfilled; "we committed X, spent Y, got Z" requires the commitment to exist before the outcome). Alignment is constitutively explicit (a contract nobody signed isn't a contract; sales can't be held to an inference).

Floor: someone who never ratifies anything keeps a useful reactive tool with retroactive reporting. That's a customer, not a failure.

Secondary risk: the party selling the credits is also the arbiter proving whether credits paid - a conflict of interest only manageable through radical willingness to display negative findings. If HubSpot leadership can't stomach the product showing "your AI spend didn't pay back this quarter," the trust architecture collapses.

## What's not solved

1. The sales "contract" framing assumes sales will engage with an artifact called a contract with marketing. Sociologically shaky. May need to wear the costume of pipeline settings they already touch.
2. L1 review load for a solo marketer is a real ceiling, and the economics quietly push toward L2 (which spends more credits). Structural conflict of interest. "Narrow the scope" (do less) must be presented with equal prominence to "grant the lane" (delegate more) as queue relief.
3. The attention-currency calibration can feel punitive. "I stopped asking because you stopped answering" is correct system behavior and bad relationship behavior. Budget changes must be announced, never silent.

# Appendix: Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Position | Durable, versioned commitment: ICP, brand voice, segment definitions, MQL criteria. The "system of record" for what's true about your business and market. |
| Bet | Time-bound hypothesis with a target, owner, credit budget, and kill condition. Replaces both campaigns and goals. |
| Play | Typed execution unit (re-engagement, launch, nurture) with benchmarked outcome and cost ranges. Minimum fields: audience, Bet, budget. |
| Ledger | Append-only record of every decision, delegation, credit spend, and outcome for a Bet. |
| Signal | Brand-clock health: trend vs benchmark, portfolio-wide, no targets. |
| Close | Weekly reconciliation ritual. What shipped, cost, returned, needs a decision, should be killed. |
| Delta | Diff since you last looked. Continuous version of the Close. Ranked by remaining agency, not recency. |
| Standing answer | Computed, never-authored status line for each Bet: pace, spend, risk, next decision. |
| Lane | A scoped autonomy grant for a specific task type. Tracked, revocable, earned through track record. |
| L0 / L1 / L2 | Manual / review-required / delegated-with-audit. Trust levels earned per task type, per relationship. |
